01

Hold & brief

Every email is held out of the inbox. Claude extracts what it asks the reader to do — links to open, places to sign in, money to send, codes to hand over. That briefs the victim; it is not the verdict.

02

Build a decoy

A Claude-invented identity with a real login and a funded Capital One Nessie customer — checking, savings and a card, seeded with history so it doesn't look brand new.

03

Detonate

In a disposable Daytona sandbox, a Playwright + Claude agent plays the recipient and does exactly what the email says, reading any one-time code out of the decoy's own inbox. You watch it live.

04

Judge by outcome

Money left the decoy, or its credentials landed outside the bank: scam, quarantined. Full compliance and nothing lost: safe, delivered.

05

Stay armed

The phished login keeps working. A canary flags any sign-in that isn't ours, upgrading the verdict from likely to confirmed and recording the account-takeover playbook.

06

Fight back bank-wide

Every mule account a scam drained to goes on a watchlist. Nessie's decision hook then declines any customer's transfer to that mule before the money settles.

Three possible verdicts

Each message ends up in exactly one place, decided by what complying cost.

Delivered

Safe

The decoy complied fully and lost nothing — no money moved, no secrets left the bank. Delivered to your inbox.

Quarantined

Scam

Complying cost the decoy money or leaked its credentials outside the bank. Quarantined — and the mule it paid goes on a bank-wide watchlist.

Held

Review

The run couldn't finish — the page was down or the sandbox failed. It stays out of your inbox until you review it.

Safety model

No real money moves and no real person is ever involved.

Disposable

Each engagement opens a fresh Daytona sandbox that is auto-deleted and never reused. Downloads are cancelled, dialogs dismissed and popups closed.

Decoy data only

Every victim is a Claude-invented identity on a Capital One Nessie sandbox account. The money is mock, the bank is original branding, and each session is scoped to its own decoy.

Built as an instrument

A $5,000 per-transfer limit, savings held above checking, and every bank operation tagged to a session and actor — the attribution that powers the canary and the playbook.
